Guidance Scorecard

Every company guides. Not every company lands where it said.

Management tells you what the quarter will look like. Three months later they tell you what it was. Nobody keeps the score, so nobody knows which teams routinely sandbag, which routinely disappoint, and which quietly stopped giving you a number at all.

This keeps the score. Here is Delta, guide by guide.

Delta has landed outside its own operating margin guidance in six of the last twelve quarters. That is not on any screen you have.

What arrives

An email, minutes after they report.

It carries the record — every guide the company gave, and where the figure landed against it — and whatever they have just guided for next. You already have the release. What you do not have is whether this management habitually lands where it said it would.

Nothing in it is a forecast. It counts. You decide what the count means.

Method

What it refuses to do.

Periods

Macy's guides the year and reports a quarter. Comparing them reads as a two-point miss ten months before the year is over. A guide is only ever scored against the period it was given for.

Basis

Walmart guided operating income growth of 7 to 10 per cent, and its release carries two answers: 26.9 per cent against last year as first reported, 17.4 per cent against the adjusted base the guide meant. The second is the honest one.

No midpoints

The middle of a range is a number management never gave. A result is reported by where it fell against the range they printed.

Scope changes

Honeywell's sales guide halved between two releases. It did not cut its outlook by half; it separated its businesses. Moves that large are named for what they are, not reported as cuts.

Held back

Where a gap is too large to take at face value, the comparison is withheld — and the fact that something was withheld is printed.
